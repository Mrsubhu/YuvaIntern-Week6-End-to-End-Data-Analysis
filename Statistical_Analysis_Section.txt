5. STATISTICAL ANALYSIS

Statistical analysis was conducted to investigate patterns and
relationships in the online retail transaction data.

The analyses performed in Task 4 included:

1. Welch's t-test:
   Used to compare average daily revenue between January and
   February 2011. The result should be interpreted alongside
   sample sizes, variability, and the selected significance level.

2. Pearson correlation:
   Used to measure the linear association between quantity and
   revenue. Correlation does not establish causation, and the
   mathematical relationship between these variables should be
   considered when interpreting the result.

3. Chi-square test:
   Used to examine whether month and cancellation status were
   statistically associated. Expected cell counts should be
   checked when assessing the validity of the test.

4. Multiple linear regression:
   Used to examine how quantity and unit price relate to revenue.
   Because revenue is calculated from quantity and unit price,
   this relationship is partly mathematical rather than evidence
   of an independent business cause.

RESULTS AND INTERPRETATION

The actual test statistics, p-values, effect sizes where applicable,
and conclusions must be taken from the Task 4 output files.
No statistical significance claim should be made without checking
